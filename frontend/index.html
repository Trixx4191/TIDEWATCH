    TIDEWATCH help

    1. Pick a study region in the sidebar. The map zooms to it and loads the flood scenario, tide gauges and historical storm tracks.
    2. Choose a storm category and sea-level-rise year. The flood-depth overlay, results and details update. Use ‹ › or the arrow keys to step through years.
    3. Click the map with Inspect on to read ground elevation, flood depth and whether that spot is connected to the sea.
    4. Filter storms by name, years, strength or landfall. The map and the Storm list tab update together; click a row to zoom to that storm.
    5. Compare all five categories, or see how flooding grows with sea-level rise, in the bottom tabs. Click a column or point to switch scenario.
    6. People tab: residents and homes in flooded areas (2020 Census), who they are (ACS estimates), the Flood Equity Index and the most-affected census tracts. Switch Compare/Trend between Area and People.
    7. Share copies a link to this exact view. Use Print, CSV/JSON or Overlay to take results elsewhere.
    8. Data (top right) shows what's built for each region. On your own computer, or as a signed-in admin, you can build, refresh or recompute regions, import a ready-made package, and follow progress there.

    TIDEWATCH is a screening tool: a static, connected-bathtub estimate on a ~90 m elevation grid. It ignores levees, waves and surge dynamics. Do not use it for evacuation or engineering decisions.

    Water level (relative to the elevation model's ≈ mean-sea-level datum) = scenario surge above MHHW + the region's MHHW − MSL (NOAA tidal datums) + IPCC AR6 SSP2-4.5 sea-level rise.

    Flooded land is at or below that level and connected to open water (8-neighbour rule). Land below the level but cut off is shown in green as low-lying, not connected and is never counted as flooded.

    Depth bands follow the NHC Potential Storm Surge Flooding map: up to 3 ft, 3–6 ft, 6–9 ft and over 9 ft above ground.

    Scenario surges are the upper bounds of the legacy Saffir-Simpson ranges (5, 8, 12, 18 ft; 28 ft for Cat 5). Real surge depends on storm size, speed, track and bathymetry.

    People = 2020 Census block populations placed on the elevation grid (large blocks spread over their land area) and counted where cells flood. Characteristics are ACS 5-year tract estimates. Counts are rounded to the nearest 10 because 2020 block counts include deliberate privacy noise.

    Limitations: SRTM is a surface model (trees and buildings read high); no levees, drainage, waves, friction or timing; sea-level rise is the global mean (no local subsidence).
